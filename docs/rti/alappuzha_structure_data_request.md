# RTI request: Alappuzha bridge, culvert and road-asset records

**Status:** drafted, not yet filed. Nothing in this repository currently claims any of the
figures below; they are the records that would let the allocation results stop being a property
of the assumptions register.

**Why it exists.** The project's allocation, deterioration, fragility, outage and cost layers all
run on assumed values because no Indian asset-level register is openly available
(`src/rra/assumptions.py`, `src/rra/cost/sor.py` — every entry tagged `assumed`). Under the Right
to Information Act, 2005 a citizen may obtain *existing* records from a public authority. This
letter asks for exactly those records, in the smallest form that would move a parameter from
`assumed` to `real`, and for nothing else.

---

## 1. Who to address it to

Send to the State Public Information Officer (SPIO) of the office that actually holds the
records, not to a general secretariat address:

| Addressee | Why |
|---|---|
| **The State Public Information Officer, Office of the Executive Engineer, Public Works Department (Bridges), Alappuzha** | Holds structure-wise inspection registers, damage reports and work abstracts for district bridges and culverts. |
| **The State Public Information Officer, Office of the Executive Engineer, PWD (Roads), Alappuzha** | Holds road-section maintenance expenditure, closure records and the road/bridge inventory for the same corridors. File a separate ₹10 application if you want both. |
| **The State Public Information Officer, Office of the Chief Engineer (Bridges), PWD, Thiruvananthapuram** | Holds state-level circulars: inspection frequency, condition-rating format, sanction norms. Ask here for the *formats and norms*, not the district data. |

Practical notes:

- Verify the current office address and the name of the SPIO from the district PWD office
  directory or the Kerala PWD / district portal before posting. Addressing by designation is
  valid; a wrong office is not fatal, because §6(3) requires transfer to the correct authority
  within five days — but a transfer costs a month, so address the right office first.
- The **Schedule of Rates** is normally published by PWD and may be downloadable; obtain it that
  way before spending an application on it. Ask for it only if the published version is not the
  one in force for these works.
- Do **not** send one application to three offices. One application per public authority, each
  with its own fee, is the clean route.

## 2. Filing mechanics

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Legal basis | §6(1), Right to Information Act, 2005 |
| Language | English or Malayalam; plain white paper, no stamp paper or form required |
| Fee | **₹10** per application. In Kerala this may be paid by **court fee stamp affixed to the application**, by **treasury remittance** (head of account `0070 – Other Administrative Services – 60 – 800 – 42`), by **cash against a receipt** in the SPIO's office, or by **demand draft**. Postal orders are not accepted by most Kerala offices. For a central-institution office (e.g. NHAI), follow that authority's own instructions. |
| Copying charges | ₹3 per A4 page; **₹75 for supply in electronic form** (Kerala RTI (Regulation of Fee and Cost) Rules, as amended by S.R.O. 790/2021). Ask for electronic supply and pay it — it is cheaper than hundreds of photocopied register pages. |
| First hour of inspection | Free; ₹10 per subsequent 30 minutes. Useful if you would rather photograph a register than pay for copies. |
| Deadline | 30 days (§7(1)); five days to transfer if the record sits elsewhere (§6(3)); 30 days to file a first appeal (§19(1), no fee), then the Kerala State Information Commission. |
| If refused | A refusal must be reasoned and must cite §8(1) or §9. "Not maintained" must be stated explicitly under §7(9); that answer is itself useful — it is a publishable finding. |

Draft in the first person, sign, keep a photocopy, and note the date of dispatch. If you post it,
use registered post with acknowledgement due and keep the receipt; the 30-day clock starts on
receipt by the office.

## 4. Field-by-field mapping to the assumptions register

Every row below is a parameter the analysis currently *assumes*. The right-hand column states
what becomes possible once the record arrives, so the request can be defended as
proportionate — and so no field is collected without a use.

| Register entry (present status) | Record requested | What it converts |
|---|---|---|
| `fragility_beta0` — baseline failure log-odds, −6 to −1 logit (`assumed`) | C7, C8: event counts by year and by structure type; B2: denominator of structures | Event rate per structure-year becomes **observed**, anchoring the intercept instead of sweeping it |
| `fragility_beta1` — condition → failure slope, 0.3–3 (`assumed`) | B3/B4 with C7: condition grade at inspection preceding each recorded failure | Fitted slope with a real covariate; the 0.3–3 sweep narrows to a confidence interval |
| `fragility_beta2` — rainfall loading slope, 0.5–4 (`assumed`) | C7 with C8: failure dates crossed against KSDMA/IMD rainfall for the same event | Fitted rain term, or rejection of the term if flood-driven failures dominate |
| `fragility_rain_ref_level` — 2–10 yr reference event (`assumed`) | C8: which return period was associated with each recorded failure | Reference event set from observed damage rather than chosen |
| `deterioration_acceleration` — 1–3 multiplier (`assumed`) | B4: two or more ratings per structure with dates | Condition transition rates estimated from the register itself; the multiplier becomes an estimate |
| `repair_effect_minor` / `_major` / `_replace` — condition steps removed (0–1, 1–3, 3–4, all `assumed`) | D11: rating at the next inspection after each repair | Repair effectiveness measured, not assumed; the three ranges collapse to distributions |
| `outage_mean_days_culvert` (3–30 d), `_minor_bridge` (7–90 d), `_major_bridge` (30–365 d), all `assumed` | C7/C8: closure date and reopening date per structure | Person-days lost stop being a function of an assumed outage; this is the single largest driver of the headline metric |
| `emergency_premium` — 1–3 multiplier (`assumed`) | D9 with E14: emergency sanction vs planned estimate for the same structure | Premium measured from sanction orders |
| `DEFAULT_BASE_COST_CRORE` placeholders — ₹0.02 / 0.08 / 0.25 crore (`assumed`) | E12 (Schedule of Rates) and D9 (actual sanctioned amounts) | ₹ per intervention becomes a real rate; benefit-cost ranking stops being scale-invariant in the assumed unit cost |
| Labour days per intervention (`DEFAULT_LABOUR_DAYS`, `assumed`) | D9 work abstracts, where crew-days or muster abstracts are part of the record | Crew-day constraint grounded in practice; otherwise it stays a stated assumption |
| Budget constraint (`budget_crore`), currently a scenario input | D10: allotment vs expenditure by year | Budget becomes the department's actual envelope, and the allocation becomes a realistic question |
| Structure inventory: 3,859 unverified OSM crossing candidates, none confirmed | A1/A2: the register of structures by type and span | The candidate set is replaced by (or validated against) real assets; "unverified candidate" disappears for matched structures |
| Structure type, span, age — currently inferred from OSM tags | A1: type, span, construction year | Structural class and age become facts, so type-specific fragility is estimable |
| Load restriction status — not modelled at all | B6: restricted structures and the orders | Adds a real intervention category the model currently cannot express |

**Fields intentionally not requested**, because PWD does not hold them and asking would invite a
refusal: hospital and school locations (health and education departments), village population
(Census/PMGSY), congestion and seasonal closure (needs a field survey), and observed flood
extent (NRSC/NDEM, restricted). Those limits are recorded in the analysis notes rather than
hidden in the letter.

## 5. If you can only file once: the minimum viable subset

Conditions, outage durations and money are what the headline result actually depends on. Ask for
these four and skip the rest:

1. **B3 + B4** — the latest and all earlier condition ratings per structure, with inspection
   dates and the rating scale.
2. **C7 + C8** — every failure, washout and closure with the dates it closed and reopened.
3. **D9 + D10** — expenditure actually incurred per work, and the annual allotment versus
   spend.
4. **E12** — the Schedule of Rates volume in force.

That combination moves five register entries from `assumed` to measured, including every outage
parameter, and it is the difference between "we assume a culvert is out for 10 days" and "the
register shows a median restoration of N days for this district".

---

## 6. What this request cannot fix

Stated plainly so the reply is not oversold:

- **Travel speeds, travel-time caps and facility weights** are modelling choices, not records.
  No department holds them; they stay swept (`speed_*`, `travel_time_cap_*`,
  `facility_weight_*`).
- **The flood depth product is modelled**, not observed. A PWD reply on 2018 damage validates
  *consequences* (which structures failed), not the depth raster's extent; the over-extent
  problem noted in the flood README remains.
- **PMGSY population vs Census denominators** are a Census/PMGSY question, not a PWD one.
- **Whether a structure *should* be repaired at a given time** is an engineering judgement the
  records inform but do not settle; the analysis must keep claiming only what the data supports.

## 7. How replies enter the repository

To keep the register honest, an answer changes a parameter's `source` tag only under these
rules:

1. Store the reply (or the scanned pages) under `data/raw/pwd/` with a `README.md` recording the
   application reference number, the office, the date of reply, and the page numbers used.
2. Change a parameter from `assumed` to `real` only when a specific page or row states the value.
   Derived quantities (rates, medians, transition probabilities) are `fitted`, never `real`.
3. Where a record is missing for part of the district, keep the assumed range and report the
   coverage gap; do not extrapolate silently from the offices that did reply.
4. Run `python scripts/ingest_pwd_reply.py data/raw/pwd/<reply> --write-docs`. The loader
   enforces rules 1–3: it refuses a reply with no provenance, tags anything it computes as
   `fitted`, needs at least five usable observations before replacing a value, and records
   every parameter it left as an assumption. It writes `docs/assumptions_measured.md` so the
   measured values sit beside the register of assumptions rather than overwriting it. The
   expected reply format is in [`reply_template/`](reply_template/README.md).
